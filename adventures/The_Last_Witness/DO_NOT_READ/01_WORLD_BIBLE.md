## 2. Setting

### Greyhaven

Greyhaven is a fictional coastal city of approximately 280,000 residents. Its shipbuilding industry collapsed twenty years ago. A redevelopment program called **Northstar Renewal** promised emergency housing, harbor repair, and commercial investment.

The city is wet, windy, and economically divided. Modern glass offices stand beside abandoned warehouses and century-old service tunnels. Public institutions still function, but many departments are understaffed and vulnerable to political pressure.

### Relevant institutions

- **Greyhaven Police Department**
- **Office of the Deputy Mayor**
- **Northstar Renewal Authority**
- **Greyhaven Ledger**, an independent local newspaper
- **St. Orison Medical Centre**
- **Port and Ferry Authority**
- **Regional Public Integrity Office**, external to city control

## 3. Corruption scheme

Deputy Mayor Mara Vale created the scheme eighteen months before the adventure.

Northstar Renewal awarded emergency contracts to three shell companies:

- Pelican Civic Works;
- Broadwater Logistics;
- Morrow Structural Services.

All three ultimately route payments to Jonas Krell's holding company. Work invoices are inflated or entirely fictional. A portion of the money is returned through political consulting fees and property transfers controlled by Vale.

Inspector Adrian Rook provides protection by:

- discouraging fraud complaints;
- classifying break-ins as unrelated property offences;
- identifying potential whistleblowers;
- leaking investigative activity to Krell;
- using camera-access powers without proper authorization.

Elias Varga discovered the pattern because several invoices reused identical rounding errors and supplier identifiers. He copied the relevant records into an encrypted ledger.

## 4. The ledger

The “ledger” is not a paper notebook. It is an encrypted archive stored on a small black hardware key resembling a thick USB drive.

### Contents

- payment records;
- internal approval emails;
- shell-company ownership documents;
- audio from one meeting between Vale, Krell, and Rook;
- Elias's explanatory index;
- cryptographic hashes matching files on Northstar servers.

### Copies

There are three technically distinct versions:

1. **Primary key:** carried by Elias and hidden inside the signal room.
2. **Encrypted upload:** incomplete, stored in Nadia's newsroom account.
3. **Decoy key:** carried in Elias's messenger bag and taken by Silas Reed.

The decoy contains genuine but insufficient documents and a tracking script. Elias intended it to waste an attacker's time.

### Access

The primary key requires:

- the hardware key;
- Elias's passphrase;
- a six-digit recovery code split between Nadia and Elias.

Nadia knows the first three digits. Elias hid the last three in a ferry photograph using marked window numbers.

### Passphrase custody and recovery

Elias set the passphrase himself and is the only person who knows it.

Elias also configured a documented recovery procedure against the primary archive. Nadia holds the written instructions for that procedure alongside the encrypted upload in her newsroom account. With the hardware key present and the complete six-digit recovery code entered, the procedure permits a passphrase reset in place of passphrase entry.

The reset is not equivalent to knowing the passphrase:

- it takes additional time to complete;
- it is logged by the archive, which downgrades the authentication of anything subsequently extracted.

Nadia holds the instructions, not the passphrase. No other named character knows either.

## 5. Elias's disappearance plan

Elias and Nadia designed the plan after Elias received a photograph of his apartment door taken from inside the police evidence office. This proved that someone within the police had accessed seized material connected to him.

The official plan was for Rook's team to transfer Elias at 18:00. Elias concluded the transfer was a trap.

His actual plan:

1. Leave the apartment before the official pickup.
2. Create delayed signs suggesting abduction.
3. Travel by foot and tram to the old ferry terminal.
4. Hide in Signal Room 4B.
5. Contact Nadia from a clean device.
6. Wait until Nadia transferred the ledger to Regional Public Integrity at 02:00.
7. Leave Greyhaven by a freight ferry arranged through Nadia's source.

Elias did not tell Nadia that he had contacted Lena. He feared Nadia would cancel the plan.

## 6. The apartment staging

Elias left at 17:42 through a service corridor behind the building laundry room.

Before leaving, he:

- overturned a chair;
- cracked a cheap phone screen;
- placed two drops of stored blood from a medical test on the kitchen floor;
- left his main coat and wallet;
- set a timed audio device to produce a crash at 18:04;
- unlocked the balcony door.

The staging is imperfect.

Objective indicators of voluntary departure:

- no fresh impact marks on the apartment door;
- the blood is older and contains anticoagulant;
- his necessary medication is missing;
- his passport is missing from a concealed envelope;
- dust on the service-corridor latch is disturbed from inside;
- the broken phone contains no biological trace from a struggle.

## 7. Ferry terminal

The old ferry terminal closed after structural damage. One modern pier remains active several hundred metres away.

Beneath the terminal is a network of service rooms. Signal Room 4B is insulated, difficult to detect, and reachable through:

- a locked maintenance stair;
- an exterior drainage access at low tide;
- a narrow cable corridor from the terminal office.

The room has emergency electricity from an old battery system. It has no reliable cellular service.

## 8. Injury event

At 19:18, Silas Reed confronts Elias and Lena on the upper service landing.

Sequence:

1. Reed demands the ledger.
2. Elias produces the decoy bag.
3. Reed notices Lena behind him.
4. Lena strikes Reed's forearm with a steel flashlight.
5. Reed grabs Lena.
6. Elias lunges at Reed.
7. Reed twists away.
8. Elias loses footing on a wet stair.
9. Elias falls approximately 2.4 metres and strikes the left rear of his head.
10. Reed takes the messenger bag and leaves.
11. Lena checks Elias. He is breathing and briefly responsive.
12. Lena drags him to Signal Room 4B.
13. Lena calls Iris Bell at 19:31.

Reed did not intentionally push Elias, but his unlawful confrontation caused the fall.

## 9. Medical reality

Elias has:

- a scalp laceration;
- concussion;
- probable epidural or subdural bleeding;
- worsening confusion;
- unequal pupils beginning around 23:40;
- declining consciousness after midnight.

Iris can stabilize him temporarily but cannot perform imaging or surgery.

Without hospital treatment, the likely fatal threshold is between 01:15 and 02:30. Exact survival varies by ending and player action, but the engine must never imply that improvised treatment is equivalent to definitive care.

## 10. Communications

### Nadia's secure channel

Nadia and Elias use a dead-drop messaging service. Messages disappear after reading. Elias's last confirmed message at 18:52 is:

> Inside. Not alone in the city. Wait for my mark.

He means that he has reached the wider terminal district and noticed surveillance. Nadia interprets “Inside” as being inside Signal Room 4B.

### Lena's phone

Lena uses a prepaid phone purchased that afternoon. It contacts:

- Elias;
- Iris;
- one taxi dispatcher.

The number is initially unknown to the players.

### Rook's surveillance

Rook has access to city cameras and police location requests. His access is powerful but incomplete. Several harbor cameras are offline.

He learns:

- at 18:21 that Elias missed official pickup;
- at 19:02 that Elias used a transit card near the harbor;
- at 20:44 that Nadia searched archived terminal plans;
- potentially earlier or later details depending on player choices.

## 11. The newspaper betrayal

The Greyhaven Ledger faces closure. Marcus Hale owes printers, staff, and lenders.

Jonas Krell offers Marcus enough money to keep the paper alive for six months in exchange for information about Nadia's investigation. Marcus convinces himself that he is selling only harmless timing information.

At 16:10, Marcus tells Krell's intermediary:

- Elias will not use police protection;
- Nadia expects contact near the harbor;
- an evidence transfer is planned after midnight.

Marcus does not know the exact hiding room or the ledger passphrase.

After Elias disappears, Marcus deletes the call record from his office phone system but cannot remove the external carrier record.

## 12. Police reality

### Honest elements

Most officers know nothing about the conspiracy. Patrol officer Mina Cho becomes a genuine ally if treated carefully.

### Compromised elements

Rook controls:

- Major Crimes intake;
- camera requests;
- witness-transport paperwork;
- two loyal detectives;
- access to the central evidence room.

Rook's goal is not necessarily to kill Elias. His preferred outcome is to recover the ledger, discredit Elias, and place him in “protective custody.”

## 13. Player role assumption

The final player books will cast the two players as independent investigators temporarily working with the Greyhaven Ledger:

- Player 1 has stronger field-investigation authority and contacts.
- Player 2 has stronger research, technical, and social access.

Neither player is an official police detective. This prevents Rook from simply ordering them away while still allowing interaction with police through Mina Cho.

The players begin with different information and may split up.

## 14. Immutable facts

The following facts must not change between branches:

- Elias left his apartment voluntarily.
- He intended to expose Vale, Krell, and Rook.
- Marcus leaked partial information.
- Reed confronted Elias.
- Elias fell accidentally during the struggle.
- Lena and Iris concealed Elias.
- Elias is alive at the start of play.
- Rook is compromised.
- The primary ledger is in Signal Room 4B.
- Hospital intervention is required for Elias's best survival chance.
- The evidence transfer deadline is 02:00.
- The passphrase, or its logged reset through Elias's documented recovery procedure, is required to open the primary archive. Neither is recoverable from the decoy.

## 15. Branch-variable facts

The following may change through play:

- whether Marcus confesses;
- whether Reed cooperates;
- whether Mina Cho trusts the players;
- whether Rook identifies the terminal;
- whether Krell's people reach the terminal;
- whether the primary ledger is copied;
- whether Elias survives;
- whether the full conspiracy becomes public;
- whether Lena and Iris are arrested;
- whether the players publicly accuse the correct people.

## 16. Tone constraints

The story should remain tense and grounded.

Avoid:

- supernatural explanations;
- genius-villain monologues;
- impossible forensic certainty;
- arbitrary betrayal introduced only at the ending;
- instant hacking of secure systems;
- violence without consequences.

Allow:

- morally compromised allies;
- incomplete testimony;
- plausible mistakes;
- institutional pressure;
- urgent but understandable player decisions.
