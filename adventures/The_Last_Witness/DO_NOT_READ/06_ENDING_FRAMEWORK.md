# DO NOT READ: Ending Framework

## Ending variables

**Non-authoritative pointer.** Ending variables are declared and owned by `LOGIC/01_WORLD_STATE_VARIABLES.md` § 9, and the medical outcome is read from `ELIAS_STATE` in § 5 of that document. Trigger conditions are owned by `LOGIC/14_ENDING_TRIGGER_MATRIX.md`. This document owns narrative outcome text only.

The variables this section previously listed are superseded by the owning declarations in `LOGIC/01_WORLD_STATE_VARIABLES.md` §§ 5 and 9. Rook exposure is held in two variables, private and public, rather than one. Public accusation is held as a target and a support level, rather than a correctness flag. Rescue control and Elias's survival are named and derived there.

## END-01: The Witness Speaks

Requirements:

- Elias reaches hospital in time;
- full ledger transferred;
- Rook exposed before he controls the rescue.

Outcome:

Elias survives surgery and later testifies. Vale resigns before arrest. Krell is detained. Rook is suspended and charged. Lena and Iris face investigation but receive substantial public sympathy. This is the strongest ending, but it should require effective cooperation and time management rather than perfect dice rolls.

## END-02: Evidence Without a Witness

Requirements:

- Elias dies or remains permanently incapacitated;
- full ledger transferred and authenticated.

Outcome:

The conspiracy is exposed, but the human cost dominates the ending. Nadia publishes Elias's final index. The players solve the case but cannot call it a clean victory.

## END-03: A Life Saved, Truth Delayed

Requirements:

- Elias survives;
- full transfer fails;
- primary key remains safe or partial evidence survives.

Outcome:

Elias cannot immediately prove the entire conspiracy. Vale and Krell deny everything. Rook may remain active. A future campaign hook exists because Elias can testify after recovery, but the case becomes harder.

## END-04: Protective Custody

Requirements:

- players deliver Elias or the key to Rook;
- Rook is not sufficiently exposed.

Outcome:

Rook takes control of the rescue and evidence. Elias may survive, but the ledger disappears. Official statements describe him as unstable or criminal. Mina may later contact the players with proof that the custody record was falsified.

This is a failure ending but must clearly follow from misplaced trust, not an arbitrary twist.

## END-05: The Public Leak

Requirements:

- Nadia publishes incomplete or unverified material;
- official transfer fails or is considered compromised.

Outcome:

Public outrage erupts, but incomplete evidence allows Vale to frame the story as political manipulation. Some guilty parties are damaged; others escape. Elias's location may become public, increasing danger.

## END-06: The Silent Terminal

Requirements:

- players fail to locate Elias in time;
- no effective rescue occurs.

Outcome:

Elias dies in Signal Room 4B. The ledger may later be found, stolen, or destroyed depending on antagonist state. Lena and Iris are blamed. The players can reconstruct what happened only afterward.

## END-07: The Wrong Accusation

Requirements:

- players publicly accuse Nadia, Lena, Marcus, or Reed as sole mastermind;
- Rook and the broader conspiracy remain hidden.

Outcome:

The accused person absorbs public attention while Vale, Krell, and Rook stabilize their position. This ending should summarize the overlooked contradictions so players understand where their reasoning failed.

## END-08: Fractured Truth

Requirements:

- two players reach incompatible final decisions;
- one secures rescue while the other releases evidence through an unsafe route.

Outcome:

Elias's fate and the evidence diverge. This ending exists specifically for two-player conflict. It should not punish disagreement automatically; in some combinations one objective succeeds while the other fails.

## Ending-quality principle

There is no requirement that both rescue and exposure succeed. The game should treat:

- saving a life;
- preserving truth;
- protecting vulnerable allies;
- and correctly identifying institutional corruption

as related but distinct achievements.

The best ending requires coordination. Several imperfect endings should still feel meaningful and logically earned.
