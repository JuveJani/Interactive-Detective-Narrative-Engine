## END_JUSTICE: Correct accusation and preserved evidence

Outcome:

The investigators prove that Dana Cole manually triggered the CO₂ purge while Elena Park was incapacitated by surprise, not by automated fault. Badge mismatch logs, manual override timestamps, and Sable Ortiz's testimony break Dana's vendor-call alibi. Elena's audit workbook and Glassline procurement records tie the murder to the embezzlement scheme.

County investigators take custody of the incident packet before the 00:30 lock. Dana is detained on campus. Marcus Hale's earlier safety cover-up is exposed separately and referred to internal review, but he is cleared of murder. Priya Nair helps stabilize Elena's unfinished validation work without being blamed for rivalry. Tom Reyes returns to maintenance duty with his dock ticket entered into the record. Vince Calder loses the Helix contract and faces procurement review, but his cooperation shortens the investigation timeline.

Meridian Dynamics cannot keep the story inside "tragic accident." The outside director postpones the Helix defense review until compliance finishes a full audit. The case is not clean—Elena remains dead—but the investigators prevented a glass-smooth lie from hardening into official truth.

## END_WRONG_ACCUSATION: False culprit named

Outcome:

The investigators accuse the wrong person—most often Marcus Hale, Priya Nair, or Tom Reyes—before the 00:30 deadline, while Dana Cole supports the misdirection with calm liaison cooperation. Corporate counsel accepts the accusation because it preserves the accident classification and confines liability to a single convenient target.

The chosen suspect is suspended or referred for disciplinary action. Dana leaves campus during the confusion or remains present long enough to sanitize Glassline records remotely. Within days, badge mismatch logs and override timestamps surface through county review, but the investigators' public credibility is damaged. Dana may still face eventual charges, yet the immediate victory belongs to institutional closure, not the players.

The ending text should summarize which contradictions the players ignored: Marcus's office alibi during the purge, Priya's server-room log, Tom's dock ticket, or Dana's impossible vendor-call timeline. The wrong accusation should feel earned from pressure and partial evidence, not arbitrary.

## END_ACCIDENT_VERDICT: Official accident accepted

Outcome:

The investigators fail to stop the incident report before 00:30 or choose not to challenge Meridian leadership without sufficient proof. The county industrial safety office receives a sealed packet classifying Elena's death as an industrial accident during authorized after-hours validation. The PLC fault export becomes the official cause.

Dana Cole departs on schedule, initiates delayed sanitization of Glassline vendor entries, and frames herself as the executive who "helped the campus through tragedy." Marcus Hale absorbs cultural blame for safety climate but avoids murder charges. Priya inherits Elena's project under grim corporate optics. Tom Reyes keeps his job but learns to avoid tunnels after dark. Vince Calder rebids the camera contract with minor penalties.

Elena's audit workbook may survive in copies if players preserved it privately, but without institutional acceptance it becomes whistleblower material rather than case closure. The players know the bay felt wrong; the record says otherwise.

## END_PARTIAL_EXPOSURE: Fraud without murder proof

Outcome:

The investigators expose Glassline Industrial Supply and Dana Cole's procurement laundering through Elena's workbook, server exports, and executive-suite documents, but they cannot validate manual override, sensor spoofing, or timeline breaks to murder proof before the deadline. Corporate counsel splits the outcome: fraud referral to compliance, accident classification for Elena's death.

Dana resigns as liaison and hires external counsel. She avoids immediate homicide arrest because the purge remains officially automated. Marcus and Vince become witnesses in the financial investigation rather than murder suspects. Priya's rivalry narrative fades beside the larger fraud story. Sable Ortiz's delayed alert may surface later as obstruction, but not in time to change tonight's packet.

Meridian Dynamics suffers reputational damage and audit cost, yet Elena is still buried under an accident verdict. The ending should make clear that the players improved the truth without completing it—a meaningful partial win with moral residue.

## END_ESCAPE: Culprit leaves before justice

Outcome:

The investigators identify Dana Cole as the likely killer or discover decisive fraud evidence, but they do not secure detention, counsel cooperation, or record preservation before 00:30. Dana exits campus citing a family emergency, triggers remote sanitization scripts, and reaches legal representation before county investigators arrive.

Physical evidence remains in fragments: partial badge logs, workbook copies, witness statements. Enough survives for a later prosecution arc, but not for immediate justice inside the adventure window. Marcus, Priya, Tom, and Vince each give conflicting interviews once Dana is gone. Corporate leadership releases a statement mourning Elena and promising "ongoing review."

The ending should not imply supernatural escape or implausible action set-pieces. Dana wins through preparation, institutional friction, and player delay—not through genre-breaking luck. If players preserved specific exports, the text notes which records might resurrect the case later; if not, Glassline vanishes into shell-company churn.

## Ending-quality principle

There is no requirement that fraud exposure and murder proof succeed together. The game should treat:

- proving homicide;
- exposing embezzlement;
- clearing red-herring suspects;
- and preserving evidence before the report lock

as related but distinct achievements.

The strongest ending requires coordination between technical log work and field timeline reconstruction. Several imperfect endings should still feel logically earned from the evidence the players actually gathered.
